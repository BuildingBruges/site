When agents lie, when communication is unreliable, when no one has a complete picture, what can we actually agree on? Byzantine consensus says: only with enough nodes. Hofstadter says: it depends on whether the other party is rational or a sociopath. The green-eyed islanders say: sometimes a tiny piece of common knowledge is the key. 

This talk connects logical puzzles to distributed system design, explaining how software systems have to make decisions with incomplete information. We'll explore how knowledge hierarchy shapes protocols, how protocols become a recipe for what agents can come to know, and how theoretical limits constrain your database replication, blockchain validation, and microservice coordination. And what to do about that. 

After leaving this talk you will probably not acquire any new skills to feed into your coding agent, but you might appreciate the inherent complexity of distributed systems more, including humans and machines.

No mathematics degree required. Just common sense.
